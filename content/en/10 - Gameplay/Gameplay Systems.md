# Gameplay Systems

LastRecall adds several rules to Terraria that directly affect how a Raid is played. This page does not list every value or behind-the-scenes detail. It covers the rules players need to understand: what happens after death, why enemies behind walls cannot be seen, how enemies notice players, and which actions take time to complete.

## Basic Raid Rules

### Loot and Extraction

Finding supplies in a Raid does not mean they are safely back at Base. They become real gains only after a successful extraction.

To extract, stay inside a valid extraction area until a short countdown finishes. The countdown does not require you to stop moving or stop fighting back, but leaving the area or dying cancels the extraction. Nearby enemies do not directly interrupt the countdown.

### Death and Recovery

Dying in a Raid interrupts a successful extraction. Items brought in or found during the Raid remain at the place of death. You can enter a Raid again later and try to return to the original place of death to recover them.

If the same player dies again during a multiplayer Raid, the new set of death drops replaces that player's previous set still on the map. Other players' drops and ordinary items on the ground are unaffected. Once the items are recovered and extracted successfully, the old drops do not return later.

### Raid Maps and Building

The original terrain and structures in a Raid cannot be freely mined, destroyed, or hammered as they can in the Main World. A route blocked by a wall is not necessarily something you can simply dig through; use the existing routes, openings, and cover instead.

Blocks placed by players during a Raid can still be removed again.

### Connected Areas

Some Raid locations have blue portals that lead to another separate area, such as the inside of the Gas Station Shop. These areas still belong to the current Raid: they do not return you to Base or start a new Raid.

You cannot know the full situation on the other side before entering. It may be quiet, or it may contain enemies and other risks. Explore the area, then use its exit to return to the main Raid map.

## Tactical Information

### Viewing Direction

Raids do not provide precise enemy information in all 360 degrees. Your viewing direction follows the mouse; the main viewing range is currently being tested at 135 degrees and may change later.

Terrain and room layouts remain broadly readable outside that view, but seeing a structure does not mean you know the exact position of enemies inside it. **Knowing where a room is and knowing who is in it right now are different things.**

### Walls and Clear Sight

Looking toward an enemy does not always mean you can see them. Opaque walls, corners, solid sloped roofs, and large solid structures can block a clear view. Doors, windows, and real gaps may provide one.

Areas behind walls provide less information so the scene better matches what can actually be seen. This is not full Fog of War: map structure remains readable, but enemies behind walls or outside your active view do not reveal their exact position for free. These extra limits apply only in Raids; the Main World keeps normal Terraria visibility.

### Sound

**Footsteps.** Enemy footsteps can indicate nearby activity, a rough left/right direction, and an approximate sense of distance. They do not provide exact coordinates. **Sound is a clue, not a radar.**

**Environmental gunfire.** The M1911 currently sounds noticeably different when fired in an open area versus an indoor or enclosed space. This sense of space can suggest the kind of area where fighting is happening, without giving away the shooter's exact position.

### Enemy Awareness and Behaviour

Scavengers are not all-knowing. They patrol and need awareness and a clear view to find players. Once they do, they pursue and fight at range. Losing sight does not make them immediately forget: for a short time, they remember where the player was last seen and continue checking that direction. Directly attacking an enemy also raises its alertness and draws a response.

Using an enemy's facing, walls, distance, and terrain to avoid or break away from a fight is a normal part of play. Enemy activity areas, pursuit, and reactions to nearby events are still being tuned for Desert Wasteland.

## Actions and Combat

### Searching Containers

Opening a container does not reveal everything immediately. Searching takes time and items are revealed gradually. You can leave or stop searching partway through.

### Injuries and Treatment

There is no natural health regeneration in a Raid. Medical items take time to finish treatment rather than restoring health instantly, so choose when to deal with injuries. Early medical and supply items are available now, with more still to come.

### Firearms and Ammunition

When holding a LastRecall firearm, the interface shows the current magazine and reserve ammunition. When ammunition is depleted, the weapon cannot keep firing normally, and an empty weapon has distinct sound feedback.

## Preparation and Results

### Equipment for a Raid

Before leaving, choose which equipment to bring into a Raid. Backpack items not taken along are kept temporarily and can be recovered from your personal recovery container after returning to Base. This is still an early, temporary way of managing equipment.

### Loot and Trading

Some Raid goods brought safely back to Base can be sold to the Recovery Broker for Terra Coin. Terra Coin can buy the firearms, ammunition, and attachments currently available. An item you do not plan to use personally may still be worth bringing back for its trade value.

## Multiplayer

### Teams

Players on the same team currently do not damage one another, can identify teammate names, and remain normally visible.

### Other Teams

Different teams can enter the same Raid and naturally meet through routes, buildings, loot, or extraction. Players can fight, avoid one another, observe, compete over resources, or choose to cooperate. PvP is a possible interaction, not the only required purpose of a Raid.

Hostile players follow the same Raid tactical-visibility rules. Their body, held weapon, and health bar should not reveal an exact position through walls or outside the active view.
