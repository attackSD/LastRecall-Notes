# Raid Gameplay

A Raid is the heart of a LastRecall outing. Players leave Base with equipment they chose, explore dangerous areas, fight and search for loot, then decide whether to extract in time and bring their gains home.

## Preparing to leave

Before leaving, players choose the weapons, ammunition, medical items, and supplies to take into a Raid. Not everything in the backpack has to be risked: belongings left behind are retained and can be recovered from the player's recovery container after returning to Base. This storage presentation is still temporary in the early version, but it makes preparation a meaningful choice instead of simply carrying everything.

## Entering a Raid

Desert Wasteland is the current main Raid map. Players move between open wasteland, buildings, and indoor spaces; the Gas-station Shop is one indoor area that can be entered now. Different spaces change how far players can see, which distances feel safe for combat, and how gunfire sounds.

## Vision, blind areas, and sound

In a Raid, the viewing direction follows the mouse. The current prototype uses a 135-degree total viewing range; this is a test value, not a final setting. The area in front stays clear while areas outside it are softly darkened, but terrain, rooms, and the general shape of the map remain readable.

To see an enemy clearly, looking in that direction is not enough: a solid wall or other opaque structure must not block the view. Doors, windows, and real openings may provide sight, while areas behind walls carry less visual information so the scene explains why an enemy is concealed. Not seeing something does not mean having no information; footsteps can suggest nearby activity and a rough direction and distance, letting players decide whether to turn and check.

These additional vision rules apply only in Raids. The main world keeps normal Terraria visibility. For the full rules, see [[en/10 - Gameplay/Gameplay Systems|Gameplay Systems]].

## Exploring and looting

Containers do not reveal everything instantly. Players need to stop and search, with contents appearing gradually. That time is part of the risk: while searching, a player might hear an enemy, spot another area, or start wondering whether it is safer to leave.

Loot matters because only goods brought back to Base become part of the next round of preparation, trading, and equipment choices.

## Combat

Firearms are the current main combat focus, but a Raid is not meant to be nonstop enemy clearing. Enemies should create short, clear bursts of danger: spot a threat, fight, judge injuries, return to quiet, then decide whether to explore further or extract.

Hit reactions, finishing-shot feedback, muzzle flash and smoke, gunshots, and dry-fire sound help players understand what just happened. Health does not naturally regenerate during a Raid, so getting hurt creates a real decision about whether to stop and treat it now.

## Supplies and treatment

Medical items take time to apply instead of restoring health instantly. Sterile Bandages, Field Dressings, and Compact Medkits are the current early medical supplies, while early food and drink items are also available. Treatment takes time and supplies are limited, so deciding whether to use them now or hold on a little longer is part of exploring.

Near the wasteland entrance, the current early version provides basic emergency supplies so a forgotten loadout does not make a Raid impossible to continue. This is an early-development safety measure and may not remain in the same form.

## Go deeper, or extract?

This is the most important Raid question. Exploring one more area may mean more loot, but it can also mean more enemies, ammunition spent, and medical supplies used. Leaving early secures what has already been found, while giving up possible rewards farther ahead.

LastRecall aims for there not to be one always-correct answer. Players weigh their loot, injuries, remaining supplies, nearby danger, and confidence before deciding what to do next.

## Death

Death interrupts the normal successful-extraction flow, so taking risks has real consequences. Players can have a chance to return to the death location and recover dropped items, but deeper exploration should never feel completely free of consequence. That is why preparation, supply management, and timely extraction matter.

## Returning to Base

After reaching Base safely, players can recover retained belongings, organise Raid loot, and prepare for the next outing. Eligible Raid goods can be sold to the Recovery Broker for Terra Coin, which can be spent on currently available firearms, ammunition, and attachments.

Base and Raids are two halves of the same loop: Base is for preparing and sorting, while Raids are where players risk resources to gain new choices.

## Multiplayer Raids

In multiplayer, other players' LastRecall guns remain visible, and their aim direction and left/right facing follow where they are looking. Hostile players follow the same Raid viewing and wall-cover rules as hostile NPCs: they only provide clear information when they are in the observer's active view and not hidden by a wall. When concealed, their body, held weapon, and health bar do not reveal their exact position; they return when valid visual access is restored.

Players on the same nonzero team currently remain normally visible and do not hurt one another. This is the current team experience, not a final long-term rule. Moving together, checking different spaces, or deciding as a group whether the haul is worth extracting with can give the same map a different rhythm.
